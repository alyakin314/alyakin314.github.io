An interest, not a credential

Quantum

I Why This Is Here

Quantum mechanics is one of the things I think about often and have never done formal work in. It has stayed a reading habit for about a decade, which is long enough that I should either publish something or admit it is a hobby. This page is the admission.

What keeps pulling me back is the size of the thing. The formalism for a single qubit fits on a napkin, and it is still counterintuitive on the twentieth pass. A qubit has exactly two degrees of freedom. Nearly every confusing number in the subject, four amplitudes, three Bloch coordinates, twelve real numbers, turns out to be bookkeeping wrapped around those two.

So I built instruments instead of writing an argument. Each one is a claim you can check by dragging a slider, which is the only kind of claim about this subject I trust myself to make.

II Three Instruments
i. Amplitudes and phase Six complex planes, twelve real numbers, two sliders that drive all of them. A third slider spins every dial forever and changes nothing you could ever measure. That last one is the whole point. ii. The measurement machine Prepare a state, point the apparatus, watch a lamp light. The probabilities depend on nothing but the angle between those two arrows. Run it a thousand times and watch the counts find the curve. iii. The machine and its matrix The same apparatus with its 2×2 Hermitian matrix beside it. An observable is not a new ingredient, it is a knob direction and two numbers painted on a dial. Turn the knob and the entries rearrange; relabel the dial and the trace moves.

Inline SVG and vanilla JavaScript, no dependencies and no build step. The 3D look is a hand-rolled isometric projection, two lines of arithmetic per point. Built with Claude, like most things around here lately.
