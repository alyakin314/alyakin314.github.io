Amplitudes and phase

A qubit state has two complex amplitudes, but you can ask for its amplitude along any of six axis states, twelve real numbers in all. Two sliders drive every one of them. A third slider changes nothing you could ever measure.

A Bloch sphere beside six complex planes, one per axis state, driven by polar angle, relative phase, and a global phase.

θ polar0°
φ relative0°
γ global0°
x = 0.00   y = 0.00   z = 1.00   |r| = 1.00
  • Spin γ. All six phasors rotate rigidly and forever. Every probability stays frozen and the sphere point does not move at all. That is what unphysical means operationally.
  • Slide φ. The point circles a line of latitude. The two Z dials stay put while the X and Y columns swing. This is a real degree of freedom that looks superficially like γ.
  • Slide θ. The point rides a meridian and every column responds.
  • Why twelve numbers, two knobs. Fixing γ is a gauge choice worth one parameter. Each column obeys p₊ + p₋ = 1, worth three more. And the X and Y amplitudes are linear combinations of the Z pair, so four of the six dials carry no new information.
